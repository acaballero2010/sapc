**Project**: SAPC IntellySys – Multi-Factor Student Failure Decision Support System

**Subject**: Expert Validation of AHP Domain Weights, GAD-7/PHQ-9 Screeners, and Multi-Factor Failure Predictors  
**Interviewer**: SAPC Research & Development Team  
**Expert Interviewee**: Registered Psychometrician (RPm) / Guidance Specialist  

---

### Key Discussion Topics
1. Validation of the 5 Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) Domain Weights (Academic: 30%, Family: 20%, Physical Health: 20%, Mental Health: 15%, Financial: 15%).
2. Clinical relevance of standardized screening instruments (GAD-7 Anxiety, PHQ-9 Depression).
3. The role of protective factors (extracurricular club engagement, hobbies, social support).
4. Addressing Philippine-specific familial stressors (OFW parents, eldest sibling caretaking burden, 4Ps beneficiary hardship).

## Verbatim Dialogue

**Interviewer [00:01]**:  
Magandang araw po, Ma'am/Sir. Maraming salamat po sa pagpapaunlak sa interview na ito. Para po sa aming thesis project—ang SAPC IntellySys—bumuo po kami ng decision support system na sumusukat sa 5 domains: Academic, Family, Physical Health, Mental Health, at Financial. Nais po naming i-validate ang theoretical at clinical soundness ng mga predictors at weights na ginamit namin.

**Psychometrician [00:45]**:  
Magandang araw din. That is very timely and relevant. In our guidance and counseling practice, we often see that academic failure is rarely an isolated problem. It is usually the tip of the iceberg—the visible outcome of underlying emotional, familial, or financial distress. I'd be glad to look at your criteria.

**Interviewer [01:30]**:  
Sa aming AHP matrix, ang Academic domain ay may 30% weight, Family 20%, Physical Health 20%, Mental Health 15%, at Financial 15%. Sang-ayon po ba kayo sa ganitong hierarchy?

**Psychometrician [02:10]**:  
Yes, that distribution is well-calibrated and clinically grounded. 
1. **Academic (30%)** remains the primary baseline because GPA, course failures, and attendance are direct, observable institutional metrics.
2. **Family (20%)** and **Physical Health (20%)** act as foundational pillars. Domestic strain (e.g. OFW absence, panganay burden) and chronic illness directly cause absences and study disruptions.
3. **Mental Health (15%)** and **Financial (15%)** capture underlying psychological distress (GAD-7/PHQ-9) and economic insecurity that erode student focus.

**Interviewer [04:15]**:  
Tungkol po sa Family domain, nagdagdag po kami ng predictors tulad ng: *Eldest child status*, *OFW parents*, *Single parent*, and *4Ps beneficiary*. Gaano po kalaki ang impact ng mga ito?

**Psychometrician [05:00]**:  
Very substantial. In Filipino culture:
- **Eldest children** frequently carry the *panganay* burden—nag-aalaga ng mga nakababatang kapatid o inaasahang maging breadwinner agad, which reduces their study time.
- **OFW families**: Even if remittances solve financial issues, the physical and emotional absence of parents often leaves children feeling isolated.
- **4Ps Beneficiary status**: It is a strong, objective proxy indicator of socioeconomic vulnerability. When combined with subjective financial anxiety, it drastically elevates dropout risk.

**Interviewer [07:20]**:  
Paano naman po sa Academic domain kung saan isinama namin ang *Extracurricular Club Participation* at *Hobbies*?

**Psychometrician [08:05]**:  
That is an excellent addition because you are not just measuring **risk factors**, you are also capturing **protective factors**. Students who belong to clubs (e.g., Robotics, Journalism, Performing Arts, Sports) build a sense of school belongingness. Even if they face difficulties at home, active engagement in school activities serves as a psychological buffer against dropping out.

**Interviewer [10:30]**:  
Maraming salamat po sa inyong ekspertong pagsusuri at validation para sa aming sistema!

**Psychometrician [11:00]**:
